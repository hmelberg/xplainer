Lecture 8 gave you the ICER. This lecture asks who looks at it, and what else they weigh.

::: new_page(title="The HTA agency tour", transition=wipe-left)

::: write
**Health Technology Assessment** — the national bodies that decide whether a drug is worth covering.

::: table
| Agency | Country | Approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NICE | UK | Explicit threshold: £20,000-30,000 per QALY |
| IQWiG | Germany | Efficiency frontier, no explicit threshold |
| HAS | France | Therapeutic benefit scale (ASMR I-V) |
| ICER | US | Voluntary, reference pricing for payers |

::: write_speak
Four agencies, four philosophies. Only NICE puts a number on the door.

::: new_page(title="Why the threshold isn't the whole story", transition=cross-dissolve)

::: write
A committee looking at a drug weighs more than one ratio:
- **Budget impact** — can the system actually afford the rollout?
- **Severity** — is the disease fatal, disabling, or mild?
- **Innovation** — first-in-class or me-too?
- **Equity** — who gets helped, who gets left out?
- **Public outrage** — will patients march in the streets?
- **Political feasibility** — will the minister survive the headline?

::: new_page(title="Budget Impact Analysis", transition=wipe-right)

::: write_speak
A cheap drug per patient can still be an expensive drug in total. Budget Impact Analysis does the multiplication.

::: math
\text{Total cost} = \text{price per patient} \times \text{eligible patients}

::: table
| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Sovaldi ICER (per patient) | ~€12,000 / QALY |
| Sovaldi list price (12-week course) | €60,000 |
| Eligible Hep C patients (illustrative) | 200,000 |
| **Total rollout cost** | **€12 billion** |
| Typical national drug budget | ~€25 billion |

::: write
Sovaldi's per-patient ICER passes every CEA threshold in Europe. Its total rollout price tag eats roughly half a national drug budget in one year.

::: message(level=warn)
CEA and BIA can disagree. Sovaldi passes CEA easily but blows up every BIA. Real decisions are made at the intersection of the two axes, not from one alone.

::: new_page(title="When CEA runs out of room", transition=iris)

::: write_speak
Multi-criteria decision analysis lets committees weight severity, innovation, and equity alongside cost per QALY. It's a way to put ethics into the arithmetic rather than next to it.

::: new_page(title="The decision flow", transition=slide-up)

::: mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[New drug arrives] --> B{ICER below threshold?}
    B -- No --> R[Reject or negotiate price]
    B -- Yes --> C{Budget impact feasible?}
    C -- No --> N[Negotiate, phase rollout, or restrict]
    C -- Yes --> D{MCDA concerns: severity, equity, innovation?}
    D -- Red flags --> N
    D -- Clear --> Y[Covered]

::: new_page(title="Check your intuition", transition=fade)

::: multiple_choice
A drug with an ICER of €15,000 per QALY — well below the €30,000 threshold — can still be rejected because...
* the total budget impact is too large to absorb
- it is too cost-effective
- CEA thresholds are illegal in the EU

::: celebrate(kind=confetti, bursts=2)
::: sound(name=success)

::: write_speak
Exactly. Passing CEA per patient is necessary but never sufficient.

::: new_page(title="What you now know", transition=cross-dissolve)

::: write
- **NICE** uses an explicit £20-30k/QALY threshold; **IQWiG**, **HAS**, and **ICER** don't.
- **Budget Impact Analysis** multiplies per-patient cost by eligible patients — and can veto a CEA-approved drug.
- **MCDA** adds severity, equity, and innovation into the weighing.
- Sovaldi is the textbook collision: great ICER, catastrophic BIA.
