::: write_speak
Lecture 3 showed demand is inelastic but not zero. Insurance amplifies that problem: once the bill is someone else's, how much more will I use?

::: new_page(title="Why buy insurance at all?", transition=wipe-left)

::: write
**Three reasons people pay premiums:**

- **Risk aversion** — one €60,000 bill hurts far more than 100 tiny ones.
- **Diminishing marginal utility of income** — the last euro you own is worth more than the first.
- **Peace of mind** — you sleep better knowing the worst case is bounded.

::: new_page(title="The catch: moral hazard", transition=slide-up)

::: write
**Moral hazard** — two flavors:

- **Ex-ante**: *"I exercise less because I know the insurer pays if I get sick."*
  (Harder to detect, smaller in practice.)
- **Ex-post**: *"I visit the ER because the copay is €10 and the alternative is my couch."*
  (Easier to measure, larger effect.)

::: new_page(title="Evidence: Oregon HIE", transition=fade)

::: write_speak
Oregon's Medicaid lottery winners used 40% more outpatient visits and 30% more ER visits than losers. Not because they were sicker — because care was free at the point of use.

::: new_page(title="Cost-sharing: the insurer's toolbox", transition=wipe-right)

::: table
| Tool | How it works | What problem it targets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deductible (€500) | You pay the first €500 each year before insurance kicks in. | Small, discretionary claims. |
| Coinsurance (20%) | You pay a fixed share of every bill, uncapped until OOP max. | Keeps you price-aware on big bills. |
| Copay (€10) | Flat fee per visit or prescription, insurer pays the rest. | Everyday overuse (ER, GP visits). |

::: message(level=warn)
Cost-sharing is nearly useless against Sovaldi's €60,000 price. Hit your €5,000 out-of-pocket maximum in one pharmacy trip. Moral hazard controls that work for ER visits collapse for high-cost drugs.

::: new_page(title="Check your intuition", transition=iris)

::: multiple_choice
Which of these is ex-ante moral hazard?
- Going to the ER because the copay is only €10.
* Skipping your flu shot because the insurer will pay if you get pneumonia.
- Shopping around for the cheapest MRI scan.

::: new_page(title="Wrapping up", transition=cross-dissolve)

::: write_speak
Insurance exists because we hate risk, but it quietly changes how much care we use. Cost-sharing patches the small leaks — and leaves the big ones, like Sovaldi, wide open.
