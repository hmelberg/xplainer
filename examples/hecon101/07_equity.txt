We've spent six lectures asking "what is efficient?" Now ask a harder question: even when something is efficient, is it right?

::: new_page(title="Two ethical frames", transition=wipe-left)

::: write
Economists and philosophers have argued about this for centuries. Two frames dominate the debate.

::: table
| Frame        | Goal                          | Priority                    | Classic thinker |
| ------------ | ----------------------------- | --------------------------- | --------------- |
| Utilitarian  | Maximize total QALYs          | Cheapest cost per QALY wins | Jeremy Bentham  |
| Rawlsian     | Maximize the worst-off        | The sickest go first        | John Rawls      |

::: write
Utilitarianism sums up all the good and picks whatever adds the biggest pile. Rawls argues you should judge a society by how it treats its most vulnerable member, not by the total.

::: new_page(title="Horizontal vs vertical equity", transition=cross-dissolve)

::: write
## Two flavors of fairness

**Horizontal equity** — equal treatment for equal need.
*Two 60-year-olds with the same heart disease should get the same stent.*

**Vertical equity** — appropriate differentiation by unequal need.
*A 40-year-old with young children and a 90-year-old in hospice have different priority.*

::: write
Horizontal equity is the easy half: treat equals equally. Vertical equity is where it gets messy — how *much* more should a sicker patient get, and measured how?

::: new_page(title="The orphan drug tension", transition=wipe-right)

::: message(level=warn, icon=pill)
**Zolgensma** is a one-dose gene therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, a rare disease that kills babies in infancy. It costs **€2 million per dose**. The ICER is around **€400,000 per QALY** — roughly thirteen times the UK reference threshold. The NHS covered it anyway. **Why?**

::: write_speak
Cost-effectiveness analysis says no, and equity says yes. The UK picked equity — a rare, severe, pediatric disease is exactly where the Rawlsian frame speaks loudest.

::: new_page(title="Seeing the tradeoff", transition=fade)

::: draw(size=420, animate=true)
line(40, 360, 400, 360), speak="Horizontal axis: how much we spend."
line(40, 360, 40, 40), speak="Vertical axis: outcomes, measured two ways."
line(40, 340, 380, 80), speak="The utilitarian line climbs fast — every euro buys the cheapest QALY on the shelf."

::: write
Left of the crossover, helping the sickest is also cheap — both frames agree. Right of the crossover, the frames pull apart, and somebody has to choose.

::: new_page(title="Sovaldi and the queue", transition=slide-up)

::: write_speak
Early Sovaldi rollouts forced the question: who gets the first doses when the budget caps supply? Sickest first, youngest first, or parents first — each frame picks a different queue.

::: message(level=info, icon=clock)
**France 2014:** reserved Sovaldi for patients with advanced fibrosis (F2+). **Sickest first.** **UK NHS:** phased rollout by severity plus age. **US Medicaid (several states):** fibrosis-stage rationing, which triggered lawsuits. Three countries, three queues, one drug.

::: new_page(title="Check your intuition", transition=iris)

::: write
Drug A adds QALYs at **€50,000 each** for a rare disease. Drug B adds QALYs at **€20,000 each** for a common disease. A strict utilitarian would...

::: multiple_choice
Pick the utilitarian answer.
* fund the common disease first — it buys more QALYs per euro
- fund the rare disease first — rarity deserves priority
- fund both equally — fairness means a 50/50 split

::: celebrate(kind=confetti, bursts=2)

::: sound(name=success)

::: write_speak
The utilitarian picks whichever euro buys the most health, regardless of who gets it. A Rawlsian would flip that answer; a pragmatic health system usually mixes both.

## What to remember
- **Utilitarian** (Bentham): maximize total QALYs — follow the cheapest-per-QALY
- **Rawlsian** (Rawls): maximize the worst-off — the sickest go first
- **Horizontal equity**: equal treatment for equal need
- **Vertical equity**: appropriate differentiation by unequal need
- **Zolgensma**: €400,000/QALY, covered anyway — equity beat CEA
- **Sovaldi**: limited supply forces an explicit queue; the frame decides the order

::: write_speak
Efficiency and equity are two different answers to the same question. Next we learn how to compute the efficiency side properly — the QALY, the ICER, and the arithmetic of cost-effectiveness.
