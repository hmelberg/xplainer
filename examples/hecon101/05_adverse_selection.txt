::: write_speak
Lecture 4 showed insurance creates moral hazard. This lecture shows something worse — without a mandate, the insurance market can unravel entirely.

::: new_page(title="A used-car dealer walks into a hospital", transition=wipe-left)

::: message(level=info, icon=car)
**George Akerlof, 1970.** In a used-car market where sellers know quality and buyers don't, good cars leave the market and only bad ones ("lemons") remain. Swap "used cars" for "health insurance customers" and you get adverse selection.

::: write
The insurer is now the buyer who cannot see quality. The customer is the seller who knows exactly how sick they are. Guess who ends up staying in the pool.

::: new_page(title="The death spiral", transition=cross-dissolve)

::: mermaid
flowchart TD
    A[Insurer sets premium at pool average] -->|too expensive for the healthy| B[Healthy people exit]
    B -->|only sicker people remain| C[Average risk in pool rises]
    C -->|costs up, break-even premium up| D[Insurer raises premium]
    D -->|now even more people priced out| B
    D -->|loop repeats| E[Market collapses]

::: write
Each arrow is a causal step, not a metaphor. The loop is self-reinforcing: every round of price hikes drives out the next-healthiest slice, which forces the next hike.

::: new_page(title="Watch the numbers walk off a cliff", transition=wipe-right)

::: table
| Year | Pool average risk | Premium  |
| ---- | ----------------- | -------- |
| 1    | Mixed (healthy + sick) | €3,000  |
| 2    | Healthiest third has left  | €4,500  |
| 3    | Only the sickest remain    | €7,000  |

::: write
A fictional but plausible three-year spiral. Notice the premium more than doubles even though nobody got any sicker — the *composition* of the pool is what changed.

::: new_page(title="Four ways to stop the spiral", transition=slide-up)

::: write
## Policy fixes
1. **Individual mandate** — everyone must buy in. *ACA (US, 2010), Swiss Santésuisse (since 1996).*
2. **Community rating** — insurer cannot price by individual risk. *EU non-life directive; ACA rating bands.*
3. **Risk adjustment** — insurers stuck with sicker pools get transfers from insurers with healthier pools. *German sickness funds' Morbi-RSA.*
4. **Reinsurance** — the state (or a pool) absorbs the most expensive cases. *Netherlands, early ACA reinsurance program.*

::: write
Each fix attacks a different step of the loop. A mandate keeps the healthy in; community rating stops cream-skimming; risk adjustment neutralises the remaining skew; reinsurance caps the tail.

::: new_page(title="A natural experiment, sort of", transition=fade)

::: write_speak
When the US repealed the ACA individual mandate in 2017, actuaries predicted 10 million people would drop coverage. Premiums on ACA exchanges rose 10 to 20 percent the following year.

::: message(level=info, icon=pill)
**Sovaldi flashback.** Adverse selection doesn't apply to one-off drug decisions like Sovaldi — but it explains why nobody would voluntarily buy insurance that covers it, unless coverage is pooled across everyone.

## What to remember
- Akerlof (1970): asymmetric information can kill a market
- Insurance version: average-priced premiums push the healthy out, sickening the pool
- Fixes: **mandate**, **community rating**, **risk adjustment**, **reinsurance**
- 2017 ACA mandate repeal: a live demonstration, with premiums rising 10–20%
