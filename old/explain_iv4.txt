::: write_speak
How do we find the true effect of one thing on another when the world is messy and full of hidden factors?

::: write
### The Problem: Endogeneity
When "X" and "Y" are linked by a hidden third variable.

::: write_speak
In a perfect experiment, we'd just change one thing. But in economics, variables are often "endogenous"—they are tangled up with unobserved factors. 

::: table(title="The Correlation Trap")
| Variable | Hidden Factor (Error) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Education | Natural Ability |
| Price | Quality / Brand |

::: write_speak
Take education and wages. If we just look at the data, does more school lead to higher pay, or do people with higher "natural ability" simply stay in school longer? 

::: wait(click=true)

::: write_speak
To untangle this, we use a "Tool" or an **Instrumental Variable (IV)**.

::: write
### Instrumental Variables (IV)
A variable ($Z$) that affects $X$ but has no direct path to $Y$.

::: draw(height=200)

::: write_speak
Think of the Instrument ($Z$) as a "shaker." It moves $X$ around, but it's totally unrelated to the hidden "Error" term. 

::: write
### The Two Requirements
1. **Relevance**: $Z$ must actually affect $X$.
2. **Exogeneity**: $Z$ must not be related to the hidden factors (the "exclusion restriction").

::: question
If we use 'Distance to College' as an instrument for 'Years of Education' to see the effect on 'Wages', what is the Relevance condition?
- Distance has no effect on wages directly.
- Living closer to a college makes you more likely to attend. ✅
- Smarter people live closer to colleges.

::: wait(click=true)

::: write_speak
We typically estimate this using **Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS)**. 

::: write
### Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS)
1. **Stage 1**: Use $Z$ to predict $X$. This gives us a "clean" version of $X$.
2. **Stage 2**: Use that "clean" $X$ to predict $Y$.

::: pyodide(title="IV Simulation")
import numpy as np
# Natural ability (Hidden)
ability = np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000)
# Instrument: Month of birth (arbitrary)
Z = np.random.randint(1, 13, 1000)
# Education (X) is affected by Z and Ability
X = 0.5 * Z + 2 * ability + np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000)
# Wages (Y) affected by Education and Ability
Y = 1.5 * X + 5 * ability + np.random.normal(0, 1, 1000)

print(f"Simple correlation X-Y: {np.corrcoef(X, Y)[0,1]:.3f}")
print("IV helps strip away the 'ability' bias.")

::: annotate(target="code", code_text="0.5 * Z", text="The 'clean' movement", color="orange")

::: write_speak
By looking only at the part of Education caused by the Month of Birth (the instrument), we ignore the parts caused by Natural Ability.

::: write
### Summary
* **Problem**: Correlation != Causation due to hidden variables.
* **Solution**: Find an instrument ($Z$) that acts as a random nudge.
* **Result**: We isolate the causal effect of $X$ on $Y$.
