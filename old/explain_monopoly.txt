::: write_speak(location="left")
Welcome! Today we're going to visualize exactly why monopolies are considered inefficient by economists. We call this inefficiency **Deadweight Loss**.

To understand this, we need to compare two worlds:

1. **Perfect Competition:** Where supply meets demand.
2. **Monopoly:** Where one seller controls the price.

Let's start by setting up our market graph.

::: write_speak(location="left")
In a perfectly competitive market, firms would produce as long as the customer is willing to pay more than the cost of production.

They would produce until **Price = Marginal Cost**.

This happens at **Point C** (). This is the **Social Optimum**. It maximizes the total benefit to society.

::: write_speak(location="left")
But a Monopolist doesn't care about the social optimum. They care about **Profit**.

To maximize profit, they look at their **Marginal Revenue (MR)** curve (the Green dashed line). This curve falls twice as fast as Demand.

::: write_speak(location="left")
The Monopolist produces where **MR = MC**.

Look at the graph:

1. They stop producing at **Q = 40**.
2. They charge the highest price people will pay at that quantity: **$60**.

Notice the difference? The Monopolist produces **less** (40 vs 80) and charges **more** ($60 vs $20).

::: new_page(title="Visualizing Deadweight Loss")
::: write_speak(location="left")
Here is the tragedy of the monopoly.

Look at the units between **Q=40** and **Q=80**.

* **Consumers** value these units *more* than $20 (the blue line is above the red line).
* **Producers** could make them for $20.

These are **mutually beneficial trades** that simply *do not happen* because the monopolist restricts supply to keep prices high.

::: write_speak(location="left")
This lost value is the **Deadweight Loss**. It is represented by the area of this triangle.

It is value that has "vanished" from the economy—neither the consumer nor the monopolist gets it.

::: question
Why does Deadweight Loss occur in a monopoly?

* Because the monopolist charges a price equal to Marginal Cost.
* Because the monopolist produces too much goods, wasting resources.

* Because the monopolist produces less than the social optimum, preventing beneficial trades.

* Because the Marginal Cost curve is downward sloping.

::: message(level="info", text="Summary: Deadweight Loss is the wealth destroyed because a monopoly sets the price above marginal cost, causing quantity to fall below the efficient level.")
